- Each allowed code stands for a cause category, and its description is the category name. Choose the category that best fits the case.
- For deaths from injury: code the external cause (Road Traffic, Falls, Drowning, Fires, Accidental Poisoning, Bite of Venomous Animal), not the resulting medical condition. Use the manner of death or reported intent to choose Homicide or Suicide. Use Other Injuries only for injuries outside these categories.
- For deaths during pregnancy, delivery or within 42 days after the end of pregnancy from pregnancy-related causes (e.g. haemorrhage, eclampsia, sepsis, abortion complications): use Maternal.
- For HIV and TB: use AIDS when the decedent is HIV positive with wasting, chronic diarrhoea or opportunistic infections, also when TB co-occurs. Use TB for prolonged cough, weight loss and night sweats without HIV.
- For cancers: use the site-specific category when the site is known, Leukemia/Lymphomas for blood cancers, and Other Cancers for other or unknown sites.
- For cardiovascular deaths: use Stroke for sudden focal weakness, speech loss or loss of consciousness, Acute Myocardial Infarction for sudden severe chest pain, and Other Cardiovascular Diseases for heart failure and other circulatory diseases.
- When a chronic condition (e.g. Diabetes, Renal Failure, Cirrhosis, COPD) drove the terminal illness, it is the underlying cause even if an infection was the terminal event.
- Use Other Infectious Diseases and Other Non-communicable Diseases only when no specific category fits.
- If the input contains very little or no clinical information, base your assessment on the most epidemiologically prevalent causes for this age group and sex.
